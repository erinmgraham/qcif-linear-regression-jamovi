1 The model

Simple linear regression predicts a continuous outcome from one predictor:

\[Y_i=a+bX_i+e_i\]

where \(a\) is the intercept, \(b\) is the slope and \(e_i\) is the residual. The fitted value is \(\hat{Y}_i=a+bX_i\), and \(e_i=Y_i-\hat{Y}_i\). The least-squares line minimises the sum of squared residuals.

1.1 Hypothesis test

\[H_0:b=0 \qquad H_1:b\ne0\]

The p-value assesses compatibility with a zero slope under the model assumptions. The confidence interval expresses uncertainty in the coefficient.

1.2 Model fit

  • \(R^2\) is the proportion of observed outcome variance accounted for by the fitted model in the sample.
  • Adjusted \(R^2\) penalises model complexity and is helpful when comparing models with different numbers of predictors.

2.1 Interpret the source result

The source reports \(R=0.315\), adjusted \(R^2=0.09\), an age coefficient of 0.604 mmHg per year and a 95% confidence interval of approximately 0.24 to 0.97. Verify all values in jamovi.

For each additional year of age, mean resting SBP was estimated to change by [coefficient] mmHg (95% CI [lower, upper], \(p\) = [value]). The model accounted for [R-squared x 100]% of sample variance in resting SBP.

2.2 Confidence and prediction intervals

A confidence interval around the fitted mean describes uncertainty in the estimated mean outcome. A prediction interval describes where a new individual observation may fall and is therefore wider.

2.3 Assumptions and diagnostics

  1. Outcome type: trestbps is continuous.
  2. Linearity: residuals should show no systematic curve against fitted values.
  3. Homoscedasticity: residual spread should be reasonably consistent across fitted values.
  4. Residual normality: the Q-Q plot should show no serious systematic departures.
  5. Independence: justify this from study design and sampling, not merely a plot.
  6. Influence: inspect Cook’s distance and investigate influential cases.
CautionCorrection to the source slides

A random-looking residual plot does not prove independence. Also avoid treating 10 or 20 observations per predictor as a universal minimum; required sample size depends on precision, complexity, effect sizes and data quality.
